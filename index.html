About the Journal

The Journal publishes student writing on any legal question worth arguing about — a case that came out wrong, a statute nobody reads, a problem in the courts or the classroom. Every piece is written by a PCC student and shaped by student editors before it appears in a volume.

Membership is open to any PCC student. You don't need to be pre-law, and you don't need to have published anything before — you need a question and the patience to revise it.

How a piece gets published

  1. Write a draft

    Start in the Journal's editor. Save as often as you like — nothing is visible to anyone else until you send it in.

  2. Send it to the editors

    When it's ready, submit it. It joins the editorial queue for student editors to read.

  3. Revise with feedback

    Editors leave notes in the margin — on a claim that needs a citation, a paragraph that isn't landing, a counterargument worth answering. You revise and resubmit as many times as it takes.

  4. Publication

    Once the editors are satisfied, the essay is published into the current volume with a citation and a place in the table of contents.
